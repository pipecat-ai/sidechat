# Round 10: the line after the agent answers, and int8 again

2026-11-20 and the night after. exp-064 to exp-073. Counts with 95% intervals. No line of TCL's set
is quoted: it is CC BY-NC and test only (SD-27).

## The question

On TCL's independent test (exp-063), rc0.3's weak spot was the line right after the agent answers.
When the other person speaks there, rc0.3 tells a follow-up to the agent from talk to the partner
poorly (AUROC 0.67). Half its false accepts were there, most of them questions.

Our corpora had a reason for that. Right after the agent answers, every question in them is for the
agent (`follow_up`) or a hold (`follow_up_by_other`). Comments to the others there never ask
anything. So a question after the agent always reads as for it.

Round 10 asked whether generated lines of the missing kinds fix this. TCL's own data cannot be
trained on.

## gen-0.5: three new kinds (exp-064)

The three kinds follow guide 0.4's rules as written (SD-28). They sit outside the default mix, so
earlier plans replay unchanged (checked on 450 of gen-0.4's plans).

| Kind | What it is | Rule | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| `people_question_after_agent` | Right after the agent's answer, someone asks the others about their own plans or choices | G7 | ignore |
| `partner_to_people_after_agent` | The person the agent answered turns to the others about it, asking nothing | G6 | ignore |
| `follow_up_later` | That person comes back to the agent's answer a few lines later, without its name | G6, unsettled | wait |

- **Size:** 5,997 conversations and 83,933 labelled lines (train 63,594, dev 5,919, test 14,420),
  including 1,787, 1,523 and 1,758 lines of the new kinds.
- **Briefs:** 98.1% of conversations keep to their brief, and none of the new kinds' lines is
  flagged off it.
- **Test split:** writer b, held out of training, wrote 400 test conversations. That gives the test
  wording no training writer produced.

## The committee check (exp-065)

Members a, b and c labelled 120 lines of each new kind from dev and test, under guide 0.4 as
written. The rule, fixed beforehand: a kind the majority reads otherwise more than 10% of the time
is left out of training.

| Kind | Majority agrees with the label | Kept for training |
|---|---|---|
| `people_question_after_agent` | **93.3%** (112/120; 87.4 to 96.6) | yes |
| `partner_to_people_after_agent` | 87.5% (105/120; 80.4 to 92.3) | no |
| `follow_up_later` | 39.2% (47/120; 30.9 to 48.1) | no |

- **`follow_up_later`:** in 66 of the 73 lines it differs on, the majority answers rather than
  holds. They read a person coming back to the agent's answer as continuing their exchange with it,
  which is also TCL's label. Member c mostly holds. Guide 0.4 does not settle this, so it is open
  question SQ-09.
- **`partner_to_people_after_agent`:** the differences come where the agent leads the session or
  has just offered something, so the person's decision is for it. The kind needs those settings
  excluded before it is used.

## Round 10 (exp-066)

**Recipe:** round 9's (realreq-0.2, the hold bar on AMI's real dev lines, dev picks that count real
lines), plus gen-0.5 without the two kinds the committee failed.
- Teachers: Ettin-150M, seeds 0 and 1.
- Students: two-tower Ettin-32M, distilled from the dev-picked teacher, seeds 0 and 1.

**The gate** was fixed before training (`student_gate.gate_r10`). A build ships only if, against
rc0.3's build of the same size, all of these hold:
1. TCL's people's lines answered outright fall, paired, with the whole interval below zero.
2. TCL's unnamed follow-ups answered or held fall at most 5 points.
3. Every other test is within its round 9 margin.

Picks are made on dev. TCL is read only for the gate and this report.

## int8 for rc0.3's default build (exp-067 to exp-070)

rc0.3's default build shipped fp32 only: its int8 kept 178 of 283 pilot decisions. On cloud CPUs
fp32 150M takes about 300 ms a line on one thread, so a working int8 build matters for deployment.

- **The cause** (exp-067, exp-068): int8 products saturating on a CPU without VNNI.
  - The container shows no VNNI or AVX-512 flags. On such CPUs, ONNX Runtime's u8 × s8 products can
    overflow.
  - Every full-range per-channel variant kept 173 to 230 decisions, whichever parts stayed in fp32.
    No single layer carries the error.
  - Reduced range, the usual remedy for that overflow, kept 264 on its own.
- **The recipe**, now in `export.py` (`int8_excluded`, `reduce_range`):
  - per channel, with reduced range;
  - each layer's MLP output product kept in fp32.

  It keeps 281 of 283 parity decisions, with a mean respond difference of 0.006.
- **The build** (exp-069):
  - Speed: 191 ms a line on one thread, against fp32's 298.
  - Size: 325 MB, against 598.
  - Dev: 98.95% right at its own thresholds (respond 0.6, wait 0.45), against fp32's 98.97%.
  - It is in R2 at `models/side/side-r9-ettin-150m-s1/export-int8/`, not on the Hub.
- **On the test sets** (exp-070), int8 at its own thresholds, against fp32's stored predictions on
  the same lines:

| Set | Lines | Same decision | Right, fp32 | Right, int8 | Others answered outright, fp32 → int8 | Others held, fp32 → int8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Duplex-MPE | 52,364 | 99.44% | 96.37% | 96.56% | 0.21% → 0.24% (93 → 107) | 0.80% → 0.63% |
| Ambient Bench | 16,222 | 99.38% | 96.89% | 97.19% | 0.28% → 0.30% | 2.83% → 2.50% |
| AMI labelled | 8,875 | 99.92% | 98.99% | 99.04% | none | 1.01% → 0.96% |
| AMI silence test | 10,434 | 99.92% | 99.45% | 99.53% | none | 0.55% → 0.47% |
| AMI as heard | 8,202 | 99.87% | 98.87% | 98.93% | 0.01% → 0.01% | 1.12% → 1.06% |
| Pilot, anonymous | 283 | 99.29% | 87.78% | 87.04% | 2.45% → 2.45% | 4.91% → 5.52% |
| SLURP heard, with the keyterm | 2,766 | 98.52% | 90.67% | 89.84% | n/a | n/a |
| TCL, anonymous | 3,647 | 99.29% | 92.87% | 93.06% | 1.94% → 2.06% (65 → 69) | 3.08% → 2.72% |

- **Within a point of fp32 everywhere.** Agreement is 98.5% to 99.9%.
- **Slightly fewer holds, on both sides,** because int8's hold threshold is 0.45 against 0.4:
  - People's lines held: fewer on every set but the pilot.
  - SLURP's unnamed requests held: 90.09% (2,437/2,705) against 90.94% (2,460), 23 fewer.
- **Outright answers to people:** Duplex-MPE answers 14 more of 44,528 outright, beyond noise in the
  paired check (-0.03 points; -0.06 to -0.01) but well under the 0.4% bar. TCL and Ambient Bench are
  not beyond noise.
- **Named requests:** the same on SLURP (48 of 61) and TCL; one more held on the pilot.

int8 is fit to offer beside fp32, at its own thresholds.

## What it means

- **Generated after-agent kinds teach what they say, but not TCL's distinction.** The model learned
  to leave questions about people's own plans alone. It still cannot tell, from text, a nameless
  follow-up to the agent from a reply to the partner. The cost elsewhere is fewer answers to
  unnamed requests.
- **Stop here on the text side of this gap.** Iterating kinds against TCL's errors would be tuning on
  a test set. The input that carries the distinction is timing: a follow-up comes right after the
  agent stops; a reply to the partner often overlaps or answers the agent. Timing input remains the
  next real step.
- **`follow_up_later` needs a ruling first (SQ-09).** The committee's majority and TCL both
  answer a person coming back to the agent's answer. Our label holds it.
- **rc0.3's models stay, now with int8 as an opt-in (released as rc0.3.1, SD-29).** On containers
  capped like Pipecat Cloud's, the default build's int8 is 1.5 times faster (303 ms against 465 at
  1 vCPU) and uses 0.3 GB less memory. agent-1x's int8 is 2.3 times faster at 1 vCPU and about the
  same at 0.5 vCPU (exp-074).
