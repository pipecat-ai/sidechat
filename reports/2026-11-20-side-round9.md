# Round 9: round 8 rebalanced

2026-11-20. exp-060 (`sidechat/pipeline/round8.py` with tag r9). Notes:
[phase-5-round9.md](../notes/phase-5-round9.md). Speakers anonymous throughout. Scorecards:
`reports/2026-11-20-side-r9*-scorecard.md`.

## What changed from round 8

Round 8 taught the models to hold real unnamed requests, but they also held more of people's own
talk and answered more of Duplex-MPE's lines for others outright. Dev also couldn't tell the 150M
seeds apart and picked the one over item 2's bar. Round 9 changes three things and keeps the rest:

1. **`realreq-0.2`:** 5,000 training requests instead of 12,000, and 1,000 dev requests instead of
   2,000. 45% are unnamed and held, 25% name the agent and are answered, and 30% name a person and
   are left alone (round 8: 15%).
2. **A hold bar when thresholds are chosen:** thresholds may hold at most 0.2% of AMI's 4,186 real
   dev lines (`choose_thresholds(max_held_real=0.002)`). Every model met it with room to spare (6
   to 8 lines held).
3. **Seeds picked with real lines counted:** dev's seed pick counts AMI's real dev lines left alone
   as well as generated dev decisions.

## What it says

- **The rebalance cut round 8's costs by about half and kept most of the gain.**
  - Holds on AMI's real lines dropped from 1.85–2.4% (round 8's picks) to 1.0–1.4%.
  - Answers outright on Duplex-MPE's lines for others are back to round 3's level for the 150M:
    0.21% against 0.22%, where round 8's pick was at 0.56%.
  - Real unnamed requests are held 87–91% as heard (round 8: 91–95%; Jev 82%).
- **Dev picked well this time.** For the 150M it picked seed 1, which meets the same 12 scorecard
  items as round 3. Seed 0 answers fewer of Duplex-MPE's unnamed requests, no more than Jev, so it
  misses item 5. For the 32M it picked seed 1, which meets 11 items: one more than rc0.2 (item 4,
  named requests).
- **What is still worse than the released builds is holds, and only holds.**
  - Every extra error on the Ambient Bench and AMI is a hold (speak only if nobody else does), not
    an answer.
  - Outright answers: AMI 0 for both picks (1 for the 32M seed 0); Ambient 44 lines (round 3: 33).
  - Holds are about 1 point higher on AMI (0.1% to 1.0%) and 1.7 points higher on the Ambient Bench
    (1.1% to 2.8%). That is still five to thirteen times fewer than Jev's.
- **Thresholds can't buy the holds back.** On the stored predictions, raising the wait threshold
  from 0.40 to 0.55 lowers the 150M's holds on AMI only from 1.0% to 0.9%. On SLURP it lowers
  requests held from 89% to 88%. The model is confident on these lines.
- **Most of the held AMI lines are questions people ask each other without a name.**
  - Of the 150M's 90 held lines, 63 are elicitations (AMI's `el.*` dialogue acts). 55 of these are
    requests for information: 10.4% of all 527 such lines, against 1.7% for round 3.
  - 40 of the 90 are said to the whole group.
  - These are the lines SD-19 left out of AMI's training and dev data as possible even calls. In
    text, without a name, a question to the room and a request to an assistant differ only in what
    is asked.
- **Duplex-MPE's unnamed requests went the other way for the 150M:** 29.3% answered against round
  3's 38.8%. That still meets item 5 (Jev 26.6%), but only just. Real requests in meetings and
  Duplex-MPE's requests that only the context identifies remain different skills (round 8).
- **By the round's gate, nothing ships.** It allows at most 0.5 points worse than the released
  build on AMI and the Ambient Bench, and 1 point more holds on AMI as heard. The 150M fails on the
  Ambient Bench (-1.83) and AMI (-0.90). The 32M s1 fails on the Ambient Bench (-0.95), AMI (-1.32)
  and AMI as heard (+1.42). I set those margins in round 8 as "no regression". The trade they
  block (about one more hold per 100 lines of people's talk, for holding nine in ten real unnamed
  requests instead of one in six) is a product call, not a measurement one.

## Cost

About $35 by run times (about 7 H100-hours with their CPU and memory), against a $28 forecast and a
$60 cap. About $17 of it is wasted.

- Modal preempted the round's job at 10:35 UTC and a teacher's `train_run` at 10:44, and restarted
  both on the same input.
- The restarted job trained both teachers a second time and launched the students a second time.
- The second runs finished last, so they are the results: both students were taught by the
  second-run seed 1 teacher.
- Fixed for later runs (`sidechat/lib/resume.py`, SD-25).
